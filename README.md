# Italian Net Salary Guide

A practical guide to understanding how gross salary becomes net salary in Italy, including RAL, INPS contributions, IRPEF, employee deductions, local taxes, and monthly salary payments.

## Introduction

When comparing job offers in Italy, the salary written in a contract is usually expressed as **RAL (Retribuzione Annua Lorda)**. This is the annual gross salary, before employee contributions and income taxes are taken into account.

The amount that actually reaches a worker's bank account is the **net salary**.

The difference between RAL and net salary is not determined by a single percentage. Several components interact, including:

- Employee social security contributions
- IRPEF income tax
- Employee tax deductions
- Fiscal benefits and the tax wedge
- Regional IRPEF surcharges
- Municipal IRPEF surcharges
- Number of monthly payments
- Employment contract and applicable CCNL
- Individual circumstances and eligible deductions

Understanding these components makes it easier to compare employment offers and interpret an Italian payslip.

---

## RAL: What Does It Mean?

**RAL** stands for *Retribuzione Annua Lorda*, or gross annual remuneration.

It is the annual salary agreed between an employee and employer before employee-side contributions and income taxes are deducted.

For example, a contract with a RAL of €30,000 does not mean that the employee receives €30,000 in their bank account during the year.

A simplified calculation starts from the RAL and progressively accounts for contributions, taxable income, IRPEF, deductions, and local taxes.

For a practical calculation, see the [Italian net salary calculator](https://nettotasca.it/).
